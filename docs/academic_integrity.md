# BDS-35 Academic Integrity & Ethical Disclosures

## 1. Ethical Data Governance & Provenance Disclosure

Scientific and engineering integrity requires unambiguous honesty regarding dataset origin, algorithm capabilities, and model evaluation metrics. The BDS-35 platform adheres strictly to transparent disclosure standards.

### 1.1 Synthetic Demo vs. Real-World Data Separation
- **Master Supply Chain Data (`data/master/`)**:
  - The supplier names, product specifications, facility addresses, and multi-tier bill-of-materials relationships used in the base demonstration are **synthetically generated demo records** designed to mimic real-world electronics and automotive supply networks without exposing confidential non-disclosure agreement (NDA) corporate supplier data.
  - Every synthetic record is explicitly tagged with `provenance: "SYNTHETIC_DEMO"` in its respective CSV row.
- **Live News Data (`data/processed/live_news.csv`)**:
  - Articles retrieved through the NewsAPI integration represent **real-world live news broadcasts** authored by recognized international news organizations and wire services.
  - These records carry `provenance: "REAL_DATA"`.
- **UI Labeling Mandate**:
  - Whenever synthetic supplier networks or demo records are rendered in the Streamlit executive dashboard, a persistent visual badge reading **`DEMO DATA`** is prominently displayed. Synthetic data is **never** presented as real.

## 2. Non-Fabrication of Machine Learning Metrics

In research and industrial prototypes, it is unacceptable to fabricate classification accuracy, F1 scores, or ROC-AUC values by training and testing on synthetic demo labels and claiming them as real-world validated performance.

### 2.1 Our Commitments:
1. **Zero Metric Invention**: We report only deterministic training loss convergence curves (e.g., Dirichlet smoothness energy) and algorithmic execution times.
2. **Semi-Supervised / Self-Supervised Paradigm**: Because real-world historical supplier bankruptcy or delivery failure labels are unavailable in open benchmarks, our GNN architectures (GraphSAGE and GAT) are explicitly documented as **inductive topological diffusion models** anchored to explainable deterministic baselines.
3. **No False Benchmarks**: We do not claim 99% accuracy on synthetic labels. Real-world predictive accuracy can only be established through deployment alongside verified enterprise ERP shipment logs.

## 3. Mandatory Model Disclaimers

### 3.1 Project-Defined Risk Bands vs. Ground Truth
> **PROJECT THRESHOLD DISCLAIMER**:
> Operational risk bands (`LOW`, `MEDIUM`, `HIGH`, `CRITICAL`) and contributing weights are project-defined operational prioritization thresholds calibrated for early warning decision triage. They do NOT represent externally verified ground-truth probabilities of default, bankruptcy, or force majeure events.

### 3.2 GAT Attention Non-Causality Disclosure
> **ATTENTION NON-CAUSALITY NOTICE**:
> Graph Attention Network (GAT) attention coefficients ($\alpha_{ij}$) quantify localized feature aggregation importance within a mathematical neural network. **Attention weights do NOT prove, demonstrate, or establish real-world causal relationships.** An elevated attention coefficient indicates mathematical graph correlation, not verified physical disruption causation.

## 4. Ethical Considerations in Early Warning Alerts

Automated early-warning systems carry ethical responsibilities, particularly regarding supplier reputation and automated procurement decisions:
1. **Human-in-the-Loop Validation**: Automated alerts generated by BDS-35 are structured as decision-support recommendations, not autonomous purchasing freezes. The `/feedback` API endpoint and dashboard triage interface are specifically built to empower human procurement specialists to confirm or dismiss alerts.
2. **Deterministic Explainability**: Black-box risk scores are prohibited. Every alert emitted must be accompanied by human-readable causal factors (event severity, proximity distance, single-source vulnerability) so analysts understand why an alert fired.
3. **Fallibility Acknowledgment**: Distance from an earthquake or port strike does not guarantee an assembly line stoppage (a supplier may have dual sourcing, safety inventory, or alternate logistics routes). Alerts must be treated as flags for investigation rather than definitive proof of failure.
